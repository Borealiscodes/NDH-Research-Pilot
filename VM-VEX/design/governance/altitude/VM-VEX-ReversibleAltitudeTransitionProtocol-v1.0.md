# ⭐ **0 — Purpose**
The **Reversible Altitude Transition Protocol (RATP)** defines how transitions between altitudes occur:

- **A7 → A8** (operational ascent)  
- **A8 → Constellation** (diagnostic ascent)  
- **Constellation → A8** (diagnostic descent)  
- **A8 → A7** (operational descent)

It ensures:

- stability  
- reversibility  
- non‑activation  
- continuity preservation  
- curvature compliance  
- gating integrity  
- aperture safety  

This protocol is the **governance layer** that makes altitude transitions predictable and reversible.

---

# ⭐ **1 — Altitude Transition Model**

Altitude transitions follow this reversible model:

```
A7 (Visibility)
   ↕ reversible
A8 (Flow)
   ↕ reversible
Constellation (Diagnostics)
```

No transition is irreversible because:

- 80D curvature is compliant  
- soft‑manifold subtraction is reversible  
- gating overrides instability  
- aperture contraction restores stability  
- continuity gradients are reversible  
- diagnostics are non‑activating  

This is the **Irreversibility Paradox** in action.

# ⭐ **2 — Reversibility Conditions**

A transition is reversible only when **all** conditions are met:

### **A. Aperture Contracted**
Prevents altitude drift.

### **B. Gating Strict**
Prevents premature flow or diagnostic activation.

### **C. Continuity Stable**
Ensures smooth propagation across altitudes.

### **D. Curvature Compliant**
Prevents orbital geometry.

### **E. Occlusion Compensated**
Prevents false dimness.

### **F. Diagnostic Non‑Activation**
Ensures Constellation tools remain observational.

These conditions form the **Reversible Threshold Standard**, now applied to altitude transitions.
