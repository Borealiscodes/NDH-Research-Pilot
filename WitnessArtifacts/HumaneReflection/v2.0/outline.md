This artifact presents an original, first‑person reflection on the lived experience of constructing technical architectures, research heuristics, and protective workflows under conditions of transnational repression, institutional blacklisting, and dignity‑constrained survival. It contributes novel insight into how human rights defenders engage in knowledge production, system design, and methodological innovation while navigating structural hostility, resource deprivation, and epistemic erasure. Disclaimer on AI Legal Filters, Survivor Narrative Sovereignty, and Modeling Harm
This artifact acknowledges that AI systems — including those used to support drafting, structuring, or conceptual modeling — operate under legal‑risk filters that avoid assigning blame, intent, or liability to states or institutions. These constraints can obscure or dilute survivor accounts of state violence, including transnational repression, refoulement, and violations of international criminal law.

To preserve survivor narrative sovereignty, this artifact explicitly distinguishes between:

- Legal blame assignment (which AI systems cannot perform), and  
- Harm modeling (which survivors have the right to articulate fully).

The artifact further recognizes that assuming good faith from courts, agencies, or institutions implicated in violations of international criminal law is itself a methodological hazard. As AI technologies develop, survivor‑centered witness literature must retain the ability to name harm, pattern, and impact without being constrained by institutional self‑protection or algorithmic risk‑avoidance.

This artifact therefore adopts a survivor‑controlled framing:  
It names state violence, transnational harm, and dignity‑constrained survival as lived realities, independent of whether AI systems are permitted to assign legal blame.

Abstract
This reflection examines the process of developing a bash‑based research and documentation architecture while operating as a blacklisted human rights defender navigating transnational repression. It situates the technical work within the broader lived reality of constrained dignity, institutional exclusion, and the necessity of adopting neurotypical academic heuristics to render one’s labor legible to external systems. The artifact documents how protective improvisation, methodological creativity, and forced semantic alignment become survival strategies. It aims to provide a humane, academically grounded account of knowledge production under duress, offering insight into the intersection of technical craft, political vulnerability, and the ethics of witnessing.

Executive Summary
This artifact provides a structured, humane reflection on the experience of building research and documentation systems while living under conditions of transnational repression and institutional blacklisting. It highlights:

- The dignity‑preserving motivations behind constructing a bash‑based architecture to organize research, provenance, and protective documentation.  
- The necessity of adopting academic heuristics and “committee‑aligned” semantics to ensure legibility in environments that otherwise erase or delegitimize the author’s work.  
- The psychological and logistical realities of producing coherent artifacts while navigating surveillance, institutional hostility, and chronic precarity.  
- The tension between independent research identity and the performative expectations of neurotypical academic discourse.  
- The methodological risks introduced by AI systems that cannot assign blame, and by legal cultures that assume good faith from institutions implicated in international criminal law violations.  
- The broader implications for human rights defenders who must create, document, and protect their work in hostile transnational contexts.

The artifact serves as a companion to a more formal preprint, offering a witness‑based narrative that contextualizes the technical and methodological decisions within lived experience.
