## ⭐ **Executive Summary**

SID Headers are the **governance‑identity spine** of NDH‑CONSTELLATION artifacts.  
They declare altitude, epoch, lineage, provenance anchors, and stability envelopes.  
Their presence — or absence — shapes how artifacts behave across:

- altitude transitions  
- lineage propagation  
- stabilization corridors  
- operator inheritance  
- cross‑layer adjacency  

This case study examines **how SID Headers emerge, propagate, and stabilize** across the NDH ecosystem, including AGL v2.0, Phase‑10 artifacts, Serenity‑Manifold substrates, and subsystem surfaces like **NDH‑ZEN‑BRIDGE**.

# 🧭 **1 — The Role of SID Headers in NDH**

SID Headers function as:

- **identity anchors**  
- **governance declarations**  
- **altitude markers**  
- **epoch locators**  
- **provenance roots**  
- **stability envelopes**  

They are the “passport” of an artifact — declaring its **place in the constellation**.

SID Headers are required for:

- governance standards  
- ontology documents  
- Phase‑10 sequencing artifacts  
- AGL v2.0 operator suites  
- sensory aperture specifications  
- Serenity‑Manifold substrates  
- constellation‑wide invariants  

SID Headers are *not* required for:

- subsystem surfaces  
- release notes  
- architecture overviews  
- bridge‑layer documents  
- interface‑integrity artifacts  

This distinction is the core of the emergent behavior explored in this case study.

# 🧬 **3 — Emergent Drift Patterns When SID Is Missing**

When SID Headers are missing from artifacts that *should* have them, three drift patterns emerge:

### **3.1 Altitude Drift**  
Artifacts lose altitude context and begin behaving as if they belong to lower layers.

### **3.2 Lineage Drift**  
Artifacts lose their provenance anchors and become untraceable across epochs.

### **3.3 Adjacency Drift**  
Cross‑layer relationships destabilize, causing:

- operator misbinding  
- geometry collapse  
- aperture misalignment  
- governance contamination  

These drift patterns do **not** occur in NDH‑ZEN‑BRIDGE because it is a subsystem surface.
