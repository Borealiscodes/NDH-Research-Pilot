## 🌑 **1 — Conceptual Definition**

A **Layered Morphology State** is the diagnostic construct describing morphology configurations that exhibit **altitude‑dependent structural layering** without invoking phase‑shift operators or altitude traversal.

Where the **Altitude Curvature Class** answers:

> “What *type* of altitude curvature is present?”

And the **Phase‑Shift Operator** answers:

> “How does the morphology *transition* between altitude phases?”

The Layered Morphology State answers:

> **“What is the morphology *doing* when its structure is layered across altitude levels?”**

It is the Helix lane’s **state‑level descriptor**, capturing altitude‑layered morphology in expressive‑clarity form.

---

## 🌘 **2 — Behavioral Properties**

- **Altitude‑layered structure:**  
  Represents morphology states distributed across multiple altitude layers.

- **Phase‑aware but non‑transitioning:**  
  Recognizes altitude layering without performing phase shifts.

- **Non‑reflective:**  
  Does not encode mirror‑plane inversion (Mirror territory).

- **Non‑adjacent:**  
  Does not encode multi‑element relationships (Weave territory).

- **Non‑soft:**  
  Does not encode gentle curvature (Arc territory).

It is the **state counterpart** to the Altitude Curvature Class.

---
