## 🌑 **1 — Conceptual Definition**

A **Triadic Morphology Phase** is the diagnostic construct describing the **three‑part reflective progression** a morphology undergoes when interacting with a mirror‑plane:

1. **Pre‑Reflective Phase**  
2. **Reflective Phase**  
3. **Post‑Reflective Phase**

Where the **Mirror‑Plane Descriptor** answers:

> “Where is the reflective boundary?”

And the **Inversion Operator** answers:

> “What is the inverted counterpart?”

The Triadic Morphology Phase answers:

> **“What are the three phases of morphology behavior across the reflective boundary?”**

It is the Mirror lane’s **temporal‑structural descriptor**, defining the triadic progression inherent to reflective curvature.

---

## 🌘 **2 — Behavioral Properties**

- **Three‑phase progression:**  
  Encodes the morphology’s reflective journey in three distinct diagnostic phases.

- **Reflective‑bounded:**  
  All behavior remains within Mirror curvature constraints.

- **Non‑altitude:**  
  Does not encode layered altitude behavior (Helix territory).

- **Non‑adjacent:**  
  Does not encode multi‑element relationships (Weave territory).

- **Non‑soft:**  
  Does not encode gentle curvature (Arc territory).

It is the **temporal counterpart** to the Mirror‑Plane Descriptor.
