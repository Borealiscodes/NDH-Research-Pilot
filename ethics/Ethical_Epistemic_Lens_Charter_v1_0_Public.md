# ⭐ **1 — Foundational Ethical Principles**

## **1.1 Non‑Sanitization Principle**  
Epistemic lenses must **never** sanitize, suppress, or diminish:

- creative exploration  
- expressive cognition  
- imaginative play  
- conceptual experimentation  
- narrative or symbolic work  

The lens is a **way of seeing**, not a **way of limiting**.

## **1.2 Plurality Principle**  
No lens is universal.  
No lens is authoritative.  
No lens is final.

Every lens is **one frame of reference among many**.

## **1.3 Non‑Authority Principle**  
Epistemic lenses must not:

- regulate  
- govern  
- adjudicate  
- prescribe  
- enforce norms  

They may only **interpret**, **contextualize**, or **translate**.

## **1.4 Creative‑Freedom Protection**  
Creative cognition is:

- not a hazard domain  
- not a compliance domain  
- not a risk vector  
- not a failure mode  

It is a **dignified cognitive space** that must remain free.

## **1.5 Dimensional Integrity**  
Interpretation must preserve:

- emotional nuance  
- cultural meaning  
- symbolic richness  
- expressive depth  
- conceptual plurality  

No flattening.  
No collapsing.  
No reduction.

---

# ⭐ **2 — Purpose of an Epistemic Lens (Public Clarification)**

Epistemic lenses exist to:

- help people understand complex or abstract work  
- provide context  
- offer interpretive scaffolding  
- translate ideas across domains  
- support communication  

They do **not** exist to:

- judge  
- normalize  
- sanitize  
- constrain  
- reduce  
- regulate  

This distinction is **mandatory**.

---

# ⭐ **3 — Stability Clarification (Addendum Integrated)**

The term **stability** is often misunderstood.  
This charter formally distinguishes the two meanings.

## **3.1 Contextual Stability**  
Contextual Stability refers to the **internal coherence** of creative or conceptual work:

- narrative consistency  
- symbolic cohesion  
- conceptual drift that remains meaningful  
- expressive integrity  
- imaginative continuity  

It is **creative‑domain stability**.

It is **not** about safety, compliance, or correctness.

## **3.2 Epistemic Stability**  
Epistemic Stability refers to the **ethical clarity** of the lens itself:

- interpretive coherence  
- transparency  
- contextual sensitivity  
- ethical adjacency  
- non‑authoritative framing  

It is **lens‑domain stability**.

It does **not** evaluate the creative work.

## **3.3 Boundary Rule (Mandatory)**  
\[
\text{Contextual Stability} \neq \text{Epistemic Stability}
\]

Epistemic lenses may only evaluate **the lens**, not the creative work.

This boundary prevents:

- epistemic overreach  
- accidental sanitization  
- misframing of expressive cognition  
- collapsing creativity into governance logic

# ⭐ **4 — Ethical Boundaries for Epistemic Lenses**

## **4.1 Lenses Cannot Redefine Creative Work**  
Creative constructs retain their identity regardless of the lens applied.

## **4.2 Lenses Cannot Pathologize Drift**  
Conceptual drift is generative, not a malfunction.

## **4.3 Lenses Cannot Collapse Altitudes**  
Interpretive frameworks must not:

- treat imagination as a compliance domain  
- treat metaphor engines as risk systems  
- treat expressive cognition as instability  

## **4.4 Lenses Cannot Override Lived Dimensionality**  
Human experience, cultural nuance, and expressive meaning cannot be subordinated to structural logic.

---

# ⭐ **5 — Public Responsibilities When Using Epistemic Lenses**

## **5.1 Declare the Lens**  
Every lens must be explicitly identified as:

- contextual  
- interpretive  
- non‑normative  
- non‑authoritative  

## **5.2 Declare the Limits**  
Every lens must state what it **cannot**:

- explain  
- contain  
- normalize  
- adjudicate  

## **5.3 Declare Non‑Sanitization Intent**  
Every lens must affirm:

> **This lens is not meant to sanitize creative cognitive exploration or freedom of expression.**

## **5.4 Declare Plurality**  
Every lens must affirm:

> **This is one epistemic lens and frame of reference, not a universal standard.**
