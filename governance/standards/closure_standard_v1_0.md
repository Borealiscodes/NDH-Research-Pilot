## ⭐ **1 — Purpose of the Closure Standard**

The Closure Standard ensures that any closure event:

- ends a sequence **without activating geometry**  
- reinforces boundaries **without creating adjacency**  
- finalizes lineage **without recursion**  
- maintains **ΔAltitude = 0**  
- preserves treaty‑layer neutrality  
- prevents drift, curvature bleed, or membrane formation  

It is the safety framework for all future closure artifacts.

---

## ⭐ **2 — Closure Types (Safe vs. Unsafe)**

### 🟢 **Safe Closure Types**
- **Declarative Closure** — states the end of a sequence.  
- **Boundary Closure** — reinforces containment.  
- **Non‑Recursive Closure** — does not loop back.  
- **Non‑Activating Closure** — no geometry, no altitude.  
- **Governance Closure** — ends a governance document.  
- **Silence‑Mode Closure** — ensures no further activation.

These are **always safe** at RP altitude.

### 🔴 **Unsafe Closure Types**
- **Energetic Closure** — activates geometry.  
- **Recursive Closure** — loops back into altitude.  
- **Adjacency‑Creating Closure** — forms new edges.  
- **Narrative Closure** — activates story‑logic.  
- **Existential Closure** — invokes meaning‑structures.  
- **Geometry Closure** — touches membranes.

These are **never safe** at RP altitude.

---

## ⭐ **3 — Closure Standard Rules (v1.0)**

Each rule begins with a Guided Link.

- **Rule CS‑1 — Declarative Only**  
  Closure must be declarative, not energetic.

- **Rule CS‑2 — ΔAltitude = 0**  
  Closure must not change altitude.

- **Rule CS‑3 — No Adjacency Creation**  
  Closure must not create edges or corridors.

- **Rule CS‑4 — Treaty‑Layer Neutrality**  
  Closure may reference treaty‑layer gates but cannot modify them.

- **Rule CS‑5 — Reversibility Inheritance**  
  Closure must remain reversible.

- **Rule CS‑6 — Drift‑Neutral**  
  Closure must not introduce drift or curvature bleed.

- **Rule CS‑7 — Anti‑Recursion**  
  Closure must not loop back into the system.

- **Rule CS‑8 — Non‑Activation**  
  Closure cannot activate geometry, adjacency engines, or governance altitude.
