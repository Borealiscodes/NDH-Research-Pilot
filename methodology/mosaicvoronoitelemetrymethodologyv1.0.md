# Methodology Statement

A Mosaic‑Based, Voronoi‑Structured Approach to Witness Documentation with Emerging Telemetry Instruments

This work adopts a Mosaic methodology, a lineage‑based approach grounded in the long tradition of Witness Literature, where individuals document systemic harm and procedural irregularities through discrete, human‑scaled fragments rather than a single monolithic narrative. The Mosaic form reflects the lived reality of bureaucratic systems: experiences do not arrive as a continuous story but as scattered, uneven, and often contradictory events that must be assembled to reveal underlying structural patterns.

To formalize this structure, the methodology draws on the metaphor of Voronoi tessellation—a geometric partitioning where each cell is defined by proximity to a point. In this context, each “point” represents a lived event, procedural irregularity, statutory constraint, or service‑access barrier. The resulting “cells” are the analytic units: self‑contained, bounded, and shaped by the conditions that produced them. When viewed collectively, these cells form a tessellated surface that reveals the contours of systemic behavior.

This approach is not a novelty. It continues a lineage within Witness Literature where testimony is preserved through fragments—letters, affidavits, diaries, case notes, procedural documents, and lived accounts. The Mosaic simply updates this lineage for contemporary administrative environments by integrating statutory analysis, procedural mapping, and documentation of service gaps into a unified but non‑linear structure.

The methodology is intentionally humble and human. It does not claim to offer a comprehensive theory of institutional behavior. Instead, it acknowledges the limits of individual perspective while preserving the integrity of each documented event. The aim is not to generalize beyond what the evidence supports, but to allow meaning to emerge through the careful assembly of discrete observations.

---

Comprehensive Instruments for Reading Mosaic Tiles as Telemetry (Emerging Framework)

Because each Mosaic tile represents a discrete event or constraint, the methodology incorporates Comprehensive Instruments designed to read these tiles as telemetry—signals that reveal how systems behave under strain. These instruments are still being formalized, but their purpose is clear: to provide structured ways of interpreting fragmented evidence without forcing premature synthesis.

The emerging instruments include:

- Procedural Drift Indicators  
  Tools for identifying when administrative bodies deviate from statutory or established procedural norms.

- Service‑Access Interference Signals  
  Measures that capture how gaps in social, medical, or institutional support shape the lived experience of administrative processes.

- Statutory‑Constraint Mapping  
  A method for overlaying legal requirements onto lived events to identify where systems fail to meet their obligations.

- Irregularity Density Analysis  
  A way of assessing how frequently procedural anomalies occur within a given administrative context.

- Human‑Impact Proximity Measures  
  Instruments that evaluate how close each irregularity lies to the core rights or protections of the individual.

These instruments do not impose a rigid analytic structure. Instead, they help readers understand how each tile contributes to the larger tessellated pattern of systemic behavior. As the framework matures, these instruments will support more formal telemetry analysis while preserving the human scale of each documented event.

---

Closing Statement

By combining Mosaic structuring, Voronoi‑inspired partitioning, and emerging telemetry instruments, this methodology provides a way to document systemic harm that is both analytically rigorous and grounded in lived experience. It respects the complexity of administrative systems while remaining accessible to readers, practitioners, and researchers seeking to understand how procedural irregularities accumulate and interact over time.
