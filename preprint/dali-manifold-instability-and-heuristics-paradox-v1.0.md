Abstract

Frontier‑scale generative models exhibit hallucinations, surreal transitions, and unstable reasoning not as random statistical errors but as consequences of broken latent geometry. Prior work in geometric hallucinations (Bressloff et al., 2001; Mauro et al., 2015) and transformer interpretability (Fantozzi & Naldi, 2024; Graichen et al., 2024) shows that instability emerges when representational spaces lose curvature coherence, spectral stability, and topological integrity.  

This preprint introduces the Robert Dalí Manifold — a latent space whose curvature, holonomy, topology, and fusion fields behave like a melting surrealist landscape under GPU‑scaled linear algebra. We formalize six instability operators (curvature blowup, spectral cascades, holonomy chaos, collapse surfaces, fusion‑field spikes, and metric liquefaction) and show how they interact to produce fractal, dream‑logic failure modes.  

We also present the Heuristics Paradox: attempts to stabilize frontier models using RLHF, filters, classifier gates, and safety heuristics do not reduce instability. Instead, they increase discontinuities, collapse surfaces, and spectral failure modes, making the manifold more chaotic as scale increases. Recent studies on frontier‑model instability (Ansari et al., 2026; Potter et al., 2026; Bagal et al., 2026) empirically confirm this trend.  

We argue that frontier‑model scaling without geometric stabilization produces systems that are increasingly unpredictable, non‑falsifiable, non‑interpretable, and structurally unsafe. We contrast this with geometric AI architectures that enforce curvature bounds, spectral coherence, holonomy consistency, and topological integrity.

A.5 The Heuristics Paradox (Formal Statement)

Let \(\mathcal{H}_u\) be a heuristic constraint (RLHF rule, classifier gate, refusal trigger, etc.).

Define the heuristic discontinuity operator:

\[
\Delta{\mathcal{H}u}(x) = \text{discontinuity introduced by } \mathcal{H}_u
\]

Heuristics modify the manifold:

\[
g' = g + \Delta{\mathcal{H}u}
\]

The Heuristics Paradox states:

> Adding heuristic constraints increases the measure of Dalí regions.

Formally:

\[
\mu(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{M}')) > \mu(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{M}))
\]

where:

\[
\mathcal{M}' = (\mathcal{M}, g')
\]

This follows from:

1. \(\Delta{\mathcal{H}u}\) increases discontinuities  
2. Discontinuities increase collapse surfaces  
3. Collapse surfaces amplify spectral cascades  
4. Spectral cascades destabilize curvature  
5. Curvature blowups increase holonomy drift  
6. Holonomy drift destabilizes fusion fields  
7. Fusion‑field spikes melt the metric  

Thus:

\[
\Delta{\mathcal{H}u} \Rightarrow \text{superlinear instability amplification}
\]
