# 🧭 Academic Originality Statement
This preprint presents an original conceptual and methodological contribution grounded in the lived experience of a blacklisted human rights defender operating under conditions of transnational repression. It introduces a novel framework—Dignity Under Constraints Architecture (DUCA)—that formalizes how individuals construct parallel, ostensibly bureaucratic or academic systems (e.g., “committee” heuristics, bash‑based governance scaffolds) to maintain agency, safety, and epistemic coherence while navigating hostile environments. All analysis, terminology, and structural models are original to the author and derived from firsthand operational experience, reflective practice, and cross‑disciplinary synthesis spanning human rights, international law, systems architecture, and socio‑technical resilience studies.

---

🧭 Executive Summary
This preprint examines how human rights defenders targeted by transnational repression construct parallel epistemic and organizational systems to preserve dignity, autonomy, and operational clarity under severe constraints. Drawing from the author’s lived experience, it analyzes the emergence of a “committee heuristic”—a protective fiction used to simulate institutional legitimacy—and the development of a bash‑based governance architecture designed to stabilize decision‑making, encode safety protocols, and maintain cognitive continuity.

The work situates these practices within broader literatures on repression, digital self‑defense, socio‑technical resilience, and dignity theory. It argues that such architectures are not merely coping mechanisms but sophisticated, adaptive systems that deserve formal academic recognition. The preprint proposes DUCA (Dignity Under Constraints Architecture) as a conceptual model for understanding how individuals under threat build structured, rule‑bound, and procedurally coherent environments to counteract destabilization.

The document aims to bridge personal narrative and scholarly analysis, offering a humane, academically rigorous account that can inform future research, policy discussions, and protective frameworks for at‑risk defenders.

🧭 Abstract
Human rights defenders facing transnational repression frequently operate without institutional protection, forcing them to construct parallel organizational systems to maintain safety, coherence, and dignity. This preprint introduces Dignity Under Constraints Architecture (DUCA), a conceptual and methodological framework derived from the author’s lived experience as a blacklisted defender. DUCA formalizes the emergence of protective epistemic structures—such as simulated committees, academic heuristics, and bash‑based governance architectures—that enable individuals to navigate hostile conditions while preserving agency.

Through reflective analysis, DUCA is positioned at the intersection of socio‑technical resilience, international human rights law, and cognitive survival strategies. The preprint argues that these architectures constitute legitimate, sophisticated responses to repression and should be recognized within academic discourse. The work provides a foundation for future empirical study, policy development, and support mechanisms for defenders operating under constraint.
