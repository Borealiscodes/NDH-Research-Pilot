# 📄 Preprint Outline (Formal)
Dual‑Use Drift, Transnational Repression, and Article 35 UN Charter: A Governance Analysis

Executive Summary
This preprint examines how monitoring architectures designed to document human rights violations can be appropriated for harmful purposes when not protected by rights‑layer governance membranes. It analyzes the intersection of transnational repression, dual‑use technological drift, and the structural blind spots of domestic courts that misinterpret governance‑altitude claims as non‑credible. The paper argues that certain classes of cognitive prosthetics, reachability engines, and monitoring systems require explicit ethical constraints and lineage‑preserving IP protection to prevent misuse by defense‑sector actors.

The analysis situates these risks within the framework of the UN Charter, emphasizing that the underlying issues fall within the scope of Article 35, which allows States to bring threats to international peace and security before the Security Council. Despite this mechanism, structural and political barriers prevent its use for human rights defenders documenting atrocities or transnational repression. The preprint proposes a rights‑layer governance membrane for NDH‑aligned ecosystems to ensure non‑weaponization, dual‑use boundary enforcement, and ethical stewardship of advanced monitoring technologies.
