Falsifiability Criteria for the Geometric Operator Theory of Transformer Synthesis

Micro‑Preprint v0.1

Abstract
This micro‑preprint presents falsifiability criteria for the geometric operator theory developed in the Snowman / Geometry Land framework. The mathematical structures introduced in Appendices A–R are conceptual and formal, not empirically validated. Here we identify measurable quantities and empirical tests that can confirm or disconfirm each component of the theory. The goal is to enable independent evaluation by providing clear definitions, testable predictions, and explicit disconfirmation conditions. This document does not claim correctness of the framework; it specifies how correctness could be assessed.

---

1. Introduction
The Snowman / Geometry Land framework proposes a geometric and algebraic description of transformer synthesis. To make this theory scientifically meaningful, each mathematical construct must produce measurable predictions. This micro‑preprint isolates the minimal math required for falsifiability and defines empirical tests for each component. The full theory remains conceptual; this document specifies how it could be evaluated.

2. Minimal Mathematical Backbone

Residual Manifold (A–B)
Residual stream vectors \(r \in \mathcal{M}\) treated as points on a differentiable manifold with non‑integrable depth×sequence directions.

Stability (C)
Residual evolution approximated by stable refinement with negative Lyapunov exponents.

Spectral Structure (D)
Attention heads decomposed into eigenmodes predicting conceptual directions.

Holonomy (E)
Parallel transport around loops yields non‑trivial holonomy, implying path dependence.

Curvature (F)
Curvature tensor \(R = d\omega + \omega \wedge \omega\) describes bending induced by attention + MLP.

Interference (G)
Head‑head commutators \([Ah, Ak]\) encode interference patterns.

Lie Algebra (H)
Attention + MLP treated as elements of a synthesis algebra.

Geodesics (I)
Residual evolution approximates forced geodesics.

Invariants (J)
Identity invariants hypothesized to remain stable under synthesis.

BCH (K)
Composition approximated by Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff expansion.

Topology (L)
Residual manifold may exhibit non‑trivial topology.

Representation Theory (M)
Residual vectors treated as representations of synthesis operators.

Operator Algebra (N)
Defines algebraic rules for combining attention + MLP.

Global Stability (O)
Operator norms remain bounded across layers.

Category Theory (P)
Transport operators behave functorially.

PDE Limit (Q)
Residual evolution satisfies a depth×sequence PDE.

Variational Principles (R)
Residual evolution minimizes an action functional.

3. Falsifiability Criteria
Each construct yields a measurable quantity:

- curvature  
- holonomy  
- Lyapunov exponents  
- commutators  
- operator norms  
- PDE coefficients  
- action values  
- Betti numbers  

Each quantity has a corresponding empirical test and disconfirmation condition.
