Abstract
This preprint examines the creation of a bash‑driven analytical and governance architecture developed under conditions of transnational repression. It analyzes how constrained actors maintain dignity, continuity, and epistemic clarity while navigating surveillance, blacklisting, and institutional erasure. Through a reflective technical narrative, the work introduces a “committee‑as‑cover” heuristic—an adaptive academic simulation used to legitimize and structure decision‑making processes when formal institutional access is denied. The resulting architecture integrates human rights methodology, distributed systems logic, and lived‑experience‑driven design principles. The preprint contributes to emerging scholarship on dignity under constraints, transnational repression, and the computational practices of marginalized defenders.

---

Executive Summary
This preprint documents the development of a computational governance architecture built by a blacklisted human rights defender working under conditions of transnational repression. The system emerged from necessity: a need to preserve analytical rigor, personal dignity, and operational safety while lacking institutional protection or recognition.

The architecture uses bash scripting as a stable, inspectable substrate for organizing research, provenance, and decision‑making. To maintain legitimacy and structure, the author employed an academic “committee” heuristic—an imagined but methodologically grounded deliberative body—to simulate peer review, distribute cognitive load, and maintain ethical clarity.

The preprint situates this work within broader patterns of transnational repression, highlighting how defenders create parallel infrastructures to continue documenting harm, producing knowledge, and safeguarding truth. It argues that such architectures are not merely technical artifacts but expressions of dignity, resistance, and scholarly continuity under constraint.
