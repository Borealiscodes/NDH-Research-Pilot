# **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

Non‑injured states have **affirmative legal responsibilities** when **Erga Omnes** and **Jus Cogens** norms are violated — even if they are **not directly harmed**.

These responsibilities arise from:

- **ARSIWA** (Articles 1, 33, 48, 55)  
- **International Customary Law**  
- **UN Charter obligations** (Articles 1, 2, 55, 56)  

This map shows how these three systems interact to create:

- **universal standing**,  
- **community‑interest enforcement**,  
- **duties to cooperate**,  
- **duties not to recognize unlawful situations**,  
- **duties not to aid or assist**,  
- **duties to bring violations to an end**,  
- **and duties to protect victims and Human Rights Defenders**.

ASCII diagrams and comparison tables are integrated directly into the analysis.

# **META ANALYSIS (Systems‑Level)**

## **1. ARSIWA: Non‑Injured States Have Standing & Duties**

### **ARSIWA Article 48**  
Non‑injured states may:

- **invoke responsibility**,  
- **demand cessation**,  
- **seek assurances of non‑repetition**,  
- **and request reparations “in the interest of the injured party.”**

ASCII:

```
Erga Omnes Violation
        ↓
Non-Injured State
        ↓
Right to Invoke Responsibility (Art. 48)
```

### **ARSIWA Article 33**  
Obligations exist **toward individuals**, not only states.

### **ARSIWA Article 55**  
Customary law continues to apply even when treaties are silent.

### **ARSIWA Article 1**  
Every internationally wrongful act of a state **engages responsibility**.

---

## **2. Customary International Law: Community‑Interest Obligations**

Customary law recognizes that:

- **Erga Omnes obligations** are owed to **all states**.  
- Violations of jus cogens norms trigger **universal consequences**.  
- Non‑injured states have **legal interests** in enforcement.

ASCII:

```
Customary Norm
     ↓
Community Interest
     ↓
Universal Standing
```

Comparison:

| **Source** | **Non‑Injured State Role** |
|------------|-----------------------------|
| Customary Law | Universal standing |
| ARSIWA | Invocation + duties |
| UN Charter | Cooperation + non-recognition |

---

## **3. UN Charter: Duties to Cooperate & Prevent Harm**

### **Article 1**  
States must:

- maintain peace,  
- suppress aggression,  
- promote human rights.

### **Article 2**  
States must:

- act in good faith,  
- refrain from force,  
- respect self‑determination.

### **Articles 55 & 56**  
States must **cooperate** to:

- promote human rights,  
- uphold dignity,  
- ensure justice.

ASCII:

```
UN Charter
   ↓
Duty to Cooperate
   ↓
Duty to Act on Erga Omnes Violations
```

# **FORMAL INTERACTION MAP (Unified)**

```
## 1. ARSIWA Responsibilities
Non-injured states may invoke responsibility (Art. 48), must respect victim rights (Art. 33),
and must treat wrongful acts as engaging responsibility (Art. 1). Customary law continues
to apply (Art. 55).

## 2. Customary International Law
Erga Omnes obligations are owed to all states. Non-injured states have universal standing
to act when fundamental norms are violated.

## 3. UN Charter Duties
States must cooperate to maintain peace, suppress aggression, and promote human rights.
They must not recognize unlawful situations and must not aid or assist violations.

## 4. Combined System
ARSIWA provides procedural standing; Customary Law provides substantive standing; the
UN Charter provides cooperative duties. Together, they create a unified responsibility
architecture for non-injured states.

## 5. Individual & HRD Caveat
Victims have direct rights under ARSIWA. Human Rights Defenders have enforcement
rights under the UN HRD Declaration. Non-injured states must protect both groups.
```
