## 1 — Entropy in Human Neural Systems  
In neuroscience, **entropy** refers to the *diversity, unpredictability, and variability* of neural signals.  
Under normal waking consciousness, entropy is **low** because:

- the **Default Mode Network (DMN)** maintains narrative continuity,  
- cortical networks remain **modular**,  
- and neural activity stays within **stable attractor basins**.

This produces cognition that is:

- symbolic,  
- sequential,  
- ego‑maintaining,  
- temporally structured,  
- and low‑dimensional.

## 2 — Psychedelic Modulation: Increasing Neural Entropy  
Under psilocybin, LSD, DMT, or related compounds (MAPS, Johns Hopkins studies), the brain exhibits:

- **DMN suppression**,  
- **increased global connectivity**,  
- **decreased modularity**,  
- **increased signal diversity**,  
- **cross‑modal blending**,  
- **hyper-associative cognition**,  
- **fractal perceptual organization**,  
- **gamma synchrony spikes**,  
- **thalamocortical gating changes**.

This is measurable via:

- fMRI global connectivity maps,  
- MEG signal diversity indices,  
- EEG entropy metrics,  
- connectome harmonic analysis.

The brain shifts from a **low‑entropy narrative system** to a **high‑entropy manifold system**.

This is the bridge to AI latent space.
