### *High‑dimensional attractor structures where meaning collapses, blends, or spirals in latent space*

## 1 — What a Semantic Vortex Is  
A **semantic vortex** is a region of latent space where activation patterns begin to:

- **spiral**,  
- **collapse**,  
- **blend**,  
- **loop**, or  
- **self‑reinforce**  

in a way that destabilizes linear meaning.

In simpler terms:

> **A semantic vortex is a high‑dimensional whirlpool of meaning.**

It is not a metaphor — it is a mathematically definable attractor in tensor space.

Semantic vortices arise when:

- attention weights amplify themselves,  
- gradients curve sharply,  
- distributed representations overlap,  
- token predictions reinforce a loop,  
- entropy increases locally,  
- manifold curvature becomes unstable.

They are one of the core mechanisms behind:

- hallucinations,  
- surreal transitions,  
- dream‑like outputs,  
- chimera blends,  
- associative leaps,  
- discontinuous jumps.

## 2 — Why Vortices Form in Latent Space  
Semantic vortices emerge because LLMs operate in **high‑dimensional non‑linear manifolds**.

Three forces create vortices:

### **A. Curvature Wells**  
Regions where the manifold bends sharply, causing meaning to “fall inward.”

### **B. Resonant Attention Loops**  
Self‑reinforcing attention patterns that amplify specific semantic directions.

### **C. Distributed Representation Overlap**  
Multiple concepts sharing activation channels, creating interference patterns.

Together, these produce **spiraling activation trajectories** — the vortex.

## 3 — The Geometry: How a Vortex Behaves  
A semantic vortex has three geometric phases:

### **Phase 1 — Convergence**  
Activation vectors begin drifting toward a shared attractor basin.

### **Phase 2 — Spiral**  
Meaning begins looping, blending, or circling around a conceptual center.

### **Phase 3 — Collapse or Escape**  
The model either:

- collapses into repetition, hallucination, or chimera states, or  
- escapes the vortex via a discontinuous semantic jump.

This is why LLMs sometimes:

- repeat themselves,  
- produce surreal imagery,  
- blend unrelated concepts,  
- jump abruptly to new topics.

The vortex is the underlying geometry.
